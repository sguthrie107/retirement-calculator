Frequently Asked Questions Planning assumptions, portfolio design, and retirement stress-test details.
Can a self-directed plan help avoid AUM fees?

Yes, when you are comfortable maintaining a simple plan. This dashboard is built to make the allocation, contributions, and tradeoffs visible so broad, low-cost funds can be managed directly without an ongoing assets-under-management fee. An advisor can still be valuable for estate, tax, insurance, or complex planning; compare the scope and total cost against the help you actually need.

How diversified is a portfolio with only a few accounts?

Account count and diversification are different things. The 401(k), Roth IRA, and optional HSA are tax wrappers; inside them, the configured index funds spread exposure across U.S. large-, mid-, small-, or total-market stocks, international stocks, and bonds. The allocations add more bonds as retirement approaches. The live holdings table shows the current weights, but diversification reduces concentration risk rather than eliminating market loss.

How is the planned retirement year used?

The planned retirement year determines when the model shifts from saving and employer-match contributions into withdrawals. Before retirement, the projection applies the saved contribution schedule and portfolio returns. At retirement, it begins the selected withdrawal scenario and accounts for configured Social Security, HSA medical spending, and rental cash flow where applicable.

What do 5%, 6%, and 7% withdrawal rates actually mean?

They are first-year spending rates applied to the spendable retirement portfolio. A 6% starting withdrawal is 20% more income than 5%, and 7% is 40% more. In the fixed scenario, that dollar amount then grows with the model's 3% inflation assumption, so the difference compounds over retirement. A lower rate preserves more recovery capacity after poor early returns; a higher rate provides more current income but leaves less margin. The guardrail option instead adjusts spending within its configured band.

Which withdrawal rate is most effective?

There is no universally correct rate. Use 5%, 6%, and 7% as comparable spending plans, then weigh the income each provides against its Monte Carlo success probability and the 10th, 50th, and 90th percentile outcomes. The most useful choice is one that covers a realistic spending need while retaining a cushion for weak markets, long life, and changes to the plan.

What assumptions are behind the Monte Carlo stress test?

Each refresh runs 10,000 return paths through age 88 using the active allocation for each account. Returns are allocation-weighted and modeled with a lognormal distribution, fat-tailed Student-t shocks, clustered volatility, and a 15% larger negative shock. The test uses 3% inflation, saved contributions before retirement, and configured cash flows. A path succeeds only when it avoids depletion and its inflation-adjusted ending net worth remains at least 10% of its first retirement-year net worth. It is a risk estimate, not a forecast or guarantee.

Why do actual balances differ from the projection?

The model uses a defined savings schedule and statistical return assumptions. Actual balances change with market performance, contribution timing, employer matches, rollovers, fund expenses, and withdrawals. The comparison table makes those differences visible so the next projection can start from real balances rather than an outdated estimate.
