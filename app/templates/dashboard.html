Frequently Asked Questions

Key planning assumptions behind the retirement projection, target year, and withdrawal settings.

How is the planned retirement year used?

The planned retirement year determines when the model shifts from the accumulation phase into withdrawals. Before that year, the portfolio grows from contributions and market returns. After that year, the projection assumes distributions begin under the selected scenario.

What does the planned withdrawal rate represent?

The withdrawal rate is the annual percentage of the modeled portfolio drawn during retirement. It is a planning lever, not a recommendation. Higher rates increase near-term income but also raise the risk of the portfolio declining too quickly in weaker markets.

Why can changing from 5% to 6% or 7% move the chart so much?

Small changes compound over a long retirement horizon. A one-point increase in withdrawals means more money leaves the portfolio every year, leaving less capital invested to recover from drawdowns and support later years.

What does the Standard Deductions toggle affect?

It switches to the scenario that uses the standard-deduction tax treatment. That changes the retirement cash-flow assumptions and can affect the sustainable withdrawal path shown in the projection and stress test.

How should I interpret the retirement stress test?

The stress test runs Monte Carlo trials to estimate how often the plan succeeds under different return sequences. It is primarily a sequence-of-returns check, so it helps answer whether the current retirement date and withdrawal assumptions look resilient or fragile.

Why do actual balances differ from the projection?

The model assumes a simplified savings and return path. Actual balances move when market performance, contribution timing, employer matches, rollovers, fees, or withdrawals differ from that planned path. The comparison table is there to surface those gaps each year.
